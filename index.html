  • Hard day Go all-out. Rest the full time between reps. Never two hard days in a row.
  • Easy day Truly easy. You can talk the whole time.

Settings

Automatic follows the school year: summer base (Jun–Aug), fall build (Sep–Nov), winter pre-season (Dec–Feb), track season (Mar–Apr), championships (May). Your coach may tell you to switch.

Where this comes from
Haugen, Seiler, Sandbakk & Tønnessen (2019), Sports Medicine – Open 5:44. Review of how elite sprinters train. Speed work happens fresh, with long rests, in small amounts; hard and easy days are separated. This is why the sprint volumes are small and the rests are long.
Charlie Francis — high/low training. Every day is either really hard (all-out) or really easy (about two-thirds effort). Nothing in between, and never two hard days in a row. This is the shape of every week.
ALTIS (Dan Pfaff, Stuart McMillan) and Jonas Dodoo. Acceleration and top speed get trained every week, all year; sessions get longer and more race-like closer to the season. Mini hurdle runs for top-speed rhythm. This is why Monday and Wednesday never go away.
Tony Holler — "Feed the Cats." Time the sprints, record the best, and stop when you start slowing down. This is why fast reps have a time box.
Petrakos, Morin & Egan (2016), Sports Medicine 46:381–400; Alcaraz et al. (2018), Sports Medicine 48:2143–2165. Resisted sprinting improves acceleration. Light loads keep good sprint form; heavy loads build pushing strength and belong in the off-season. This is why summer uses heavy ExerGenie work and the rest of the year uses light resistance.
Boo Schexnayder — jump progressions. Jumps start small and in place, then short hops, then bounding. This is why the jumps change through the year.
Petersen et al. (2011), American Journal of Sports Medicine 39:2296–2303. Nordic hamstring curls cut hamstring injuries substantially. This is why Nordics show up all year.
Spencer & Gastin (2001), Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 33:157–162. The 400 is roughly 40% aerobic, far more than the 100 or 200. This is why 400 runners get more easy running and longer Friday reps.
Lloyd et al. (2016), NSCA position statement on long-term athletic development. Strength training and jumping are safe and effective for teenagers when technique comes first and loads build gradually. This is why lifting is built around common weight-room lifts, with no Olympic lifts.
